Software Development Instructions



Online Shop | Warehouse Management System | Drone Control Model
Built for a wider West African market and a Ghana-origin export engine
A build brief for the engineering team





Second draft. Strictly Private and Confidential

1. Purpose and Scope

This brief tells the engineering team what to build. It covers three systems that share one platform: the online shop that customers use, the warehouse management system that runs fulfilment, and the drone control model that governs autonomous delivery. This second draft widens the platform from Ghana and Nigeria to a wider West African market, and adds a Ghana-origin export engine. The old free-zone seventy-thirty split is removed and replaced by an origin and duty engine.
This is a functional and technical instruction, not a line-by-line design. Each team lead should turn the sections below into detailed tickets. Where a rule is marked as a guardrail, it is not optional. Several guardrails exist because the law requires them.
2. Architecture Overview and Shared Standards

	•	One client codebase. Build the mobile application on a single cross-platform codebase, either Flutter or React Native, chosen once and kept. Build the web store to share the same design system and application programming interfaces.
	•	Microservices backend. Split the backend by domain, in Python with Fast API and in Node.js. Services talk through a gateway for synchronous calls and an event bus for asynchronous events.
	•	Multi-tenant data. Use PostgreSQL with strict tenant isolation, one schema or database per tenant. Enforce isolation at the data layer, not only in application code.
	•	Multi-country, multi-currency, multi-jurisdiction from the start. Model country, currency, language, tax and duty as first-class configuration, not hard-coded assumptions. The platform must run Ghana, Nigeria and the francophone CFA-franc markets side by side, each with its own tax rules, duty tables, currency and language.
	•	Data residency. Host in the Amazon Web Services Africa region in Cape Town. Keep personal data in region and confirm the region choice against the data protection law of each market entered.
	•	Idempotency and events. Make every write endpoint idempotent. Publish domain events so the shop, warehouse and drone systems stay in step without tight coupling.
	•	Contracts first. Define each service interface as a versioned contract and never break a published contract without a version bump.
3. Cross-Cutting Requirements

3.1 Identity and access
One identity service with role-based access control across every surface. Roles include consumer, business buyer, warehouse operator, dispatcher, drone pilot or safety officer, ground courier, finance, and administrator. The business buyer role is unlocked only after the business registry check passes.
3.2 Country, currency and tax configuration
	•	Hold each market's currency, language, VAT rate, duty tables and invoicing rules as configuration a non-engineer can maintain.
	•	Price, charge and settle in the local currency of each market: cedi, naira and CFA franc, extensible to others.
	•	Apply the correct value-added tax and electronic-invoicing rules per market, starting with Ghana's electronic VAT.
3.3 Data protection and security
	•	Comply with the data protection law of each market, including Ghana's Data Protection Act, 2012 and Nigeria's Data Protection Act, 2023.
	•	Encrypt data in transit and at rest, manage secrets in a vault, and tokenise cards so raw card data is never stored.
	•	Log every privileged action to a tamper-evident audit trail.
3.4 Delivery discipline and resilience
	•	Continuous integration and deployment with automated tests as a merge gate; separate development, staging and production.
	•	Observability from day one: structured logs, metrics, tracing and alerts.
	•	Assume patchy connectivity. Customer, scanner and courier applications must work offline and sync. The drone control model must never depend on a live consumer connection to fly safely.

5.1 Origin and duty guardrail
Guardrail, replaces the old seventy-thirty rule.  The platform no longer polices a free-zone export quota. Instead, it must get origin and duty right. For every line, the system records whether the goods are third-country imports, on which the correct local duty and tax are charged in the market of sale, or Ghana-origin goods that qualify for duty-free movement under ECOWAS and AfCFTA rules. Two hard stops. First, only goods with genuine, evidenced Ghanaian transformation may be flagged as originating; simple labelling or repackaging must never set the origin flag. Second, a certificate of origin may be issued only where the value-addition record supports it. Keep imported and Ghana-origin stock distinguishable at all times.

6. System C: Drone Control Model

The drone control model governs autonomous cargo delivery from warehouse rooftops to rural and suburban lockboxes, with a ground courier completing the final leg. Safety and the law come first. Read the guardrail before the modules.
Regulatory guardrail, read first.  Autonomous flight beyond visual line of sight is tightly restricted and not routinely permitted. Build the system so every flight is gated by a valid civil aviation authorization in the country of flight, a remote pilot or safety officer can take manual control at any moment, and the default fallback is a visual-line-of-sight or ground-courier route. Enforce geofencing that keeps drones out of airport exclusion zones, restricted airspace and banned urban corridors. Do not ship autonomous urban delivery without the aviation authority's written approval in that market. The software must refuse to dispatch where authorization, airspace or weather are not satisfied.
Module
What it must do
Fleet and asset management
Register each drone, its airworthiness, battery health, maintenance log and authorization status. Ground any aircraft that fails a check.
Eligibility and weight router
At checkout, compute cart weight. If the basket exceeds the payload limit, hide the drone option and default to a ground courier dispatch ticket.
Weather lockout
Poll civil aviation weather feeds. If wind or rain thresholds are exceeded, disable the drone option and reroute to ground couriers automatically.
Authorization gate
Before any dispatch, confirm a valid flight authorization covers the route, airspace and time in that country. No authorization, no flight.
Route planning and mapping
Use Mapbox for the consumer view and three-dimensional paths, Google geocoding in the background to turn landmarks into coordinates, and internal OpenStreetMap tiles for high-frequency location pings at zero backend map cost.
Autonomous flight and failsafe
Fly planned straight-line routes with continuous telemetry. On any fault, loss of link or geofence breach, execute a safe failsafe.
Micro-hub lockboxes
Manage secure lockboxes at rural and suburban hubs, with drop confirmation and courier handover.
Ground courier handover
Dispatch the ground bike fleet for the final leg, with proof of delivery and, in volatile-currency markets, the cash-on-delivery escrow wallet.
Telemetry and black box
Record a tamper-evident flight log for every sortie for audit and incident review.
Manual override
Give the remote pilot or safety officer immediate manual control and a hard stop at all times.
6.1 Cash on delivery and currency guardrail
Guardrail.  For cash on delivery, the courier application must enforce a pre-funded escrow wallet, lock the wallet on cash collection, require deposit at an agent terminal within a set window, and route any currency conversion only through a licensed payment partner. This matters most in Nigeria, where the naira is volatile. In the francophone CFA-franc markets the currency is pegged to the euro and convertible, so the pressure is lower, but the escrow discipline stays. Never build an in-house cross-border currency conversion rail; that is a regulated activity.
6.2 Key data entities
drone, airworthiness check, flight authorization, geofence, weather reading, sortie, route, waypoint, lockbox, drop, courier, escrow wallet, proof of delivery, telemetry log
